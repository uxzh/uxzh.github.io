CardsClarity

Feb 2024 – late 2025

CardsClarity started as a cashback optimizer and became a credit card review site.

Role
Co-founder and CTO. I led 6 engineers, built the front end and rebuilt the home page for the pivot.
Team
6 engineers
Status
Shut down in late 2025
Result
19,000 visitors. Finalist in the New York Business Plan Competition.

Problem

Every credit card pays a different rate on groceries, gas and travel, and annual fees change the math. By our estimate the average US cardholder earns 1 to 2% cashback, while the best issuer deals pass 20%.

The first version had 4 requirements:

  • Tell users which of their cards pays the most for each purchase, and which new cards are worth getting.
  • No bank logins. Users add their cards by hand.
  • Never sell user data.
  • Rankings stay independent of card issuers.

Key decisions

Promised 4 tools before building one

I built the landing page, and we opened a beta waitlist in February 2024. The page promised 4 tools, all marked "coming soon".

Prototyped a cashback wallet

By July I had built a card rating table, a wallet prototype that ranked your own cards by cashback rate, and a page for each card.

Pivoted from our algorithm to cardholder reviews

In October 2024 we pivoted to a credit card review site, where you search for a card, read its details and reviews, and rate it. Customer interviews pushed us there. People trusted reviews from other cardholders, the way they trust Yelp, more than our own ranking algorithm.

Kept thinly reviewed cards off the top

Each card's score was its average rating, pulled toward 2.5 stars as if it had 3 extra neutral reviews, so a card with 2 five-star reviews couldn't top the list.

Bet on referral revenue

Revenue was meant to come from referral links on each card's Apply button. That was hard to reach, because banks only partner with sites that already have traction and the right connections.

Results

The site drew 19,000 visitors. CardsClarity was a finalist in the New York Business Plan Competition, and we pitched to investors through Blackstone LaunchPad.

What I'd do differently

Referral revenue only pays at a scale we couldn't reach without bank partnerships, and banks don't partner until you have that scale. I shut CardsClarity down in late 2025, once that was clear.

We promised 4 tools on the landing page before validating any of them. Next time I'd validate the market positioning and the revenue model before building, and interview many more people, earlier.

Our positioning promised rankings free of issuer influence, but the only way to make money ran through issuer referral links.

A review site also has a cold-start problem. It's only useful after enough people have written reviews, and our ranking had to discount every card that had just a few.
